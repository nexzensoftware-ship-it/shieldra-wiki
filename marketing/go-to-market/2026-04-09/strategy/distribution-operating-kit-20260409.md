## 1. Non-Negotiables

Use these rules in every outbound and content touch:

- CTA is **Free HIPAA Risk Assessment**
- Keep the public motion **HIPAA-only**
- Do **not** drift into Part 2, behavioral-health-specific messaging, or BA-monitoring claims
- Do **not** use unreviewed “30 days guaranteed” language
- Do **not** do creepy breach-chaser outreach
- Do **not** pitch every framework to everyone

The tone should be:
- practical
- calm
- credible
- useful
- not fear-mongering, but not soft either

## 3. Outreach Structure

Use 3 lanes:

### Lane 1 — Warm outreach
Best first motion.
Ask for:
- feedback
- intro
- quick look at ShieldRA
- assessment share

### Lane 2 — Direct cold-ish outreach
Used after list quality is good.
Keep it short and plain.
One pain, one relevance reason, one CTA.

### Lane 3 — Partner outreach
Highest leverage once it starts working.
Do not lead with “affiliate program.”
Lead with:
- fit
- shared customer pain
- useful co-branded entry point

## C. Partner call framing
If they take a meeting, the call should aim to answer:
- who do they already serve?
- where do their smaller clients get stuck?
- do they want intros, co-branded education, referral fees, or just useful tooling?
- what would make this easy for them to mention to clients?

Do not overcomplicate this. The first win is a small number of warm introductions.

## 6. First 3 LinkedIn Posts

## Post 1 — Small-practice pain
Most HIPAA tools are built like the buyer has a security team.

Most small practices do not.

They have an office manager, a practice owner, a pile of vendor docs, and a vague fear that they’re one bad incident away from a very expensive mess.

That’s the gap we’re trying to close with ShieldRA AI.

Not “enterprise GRC for healthcare.”
Not another giant dashboard.

Just a clearer way for small healthcare practices to understand where they stand, what matters first, and what to fix next.

If you’re in a small practice and HIPAA still feels more confusing than manageable, that’s exactly the problem we’re working on.

---

## Post 2 — Practical lesson post
A lot of small practices think HIPAA risk means one big dramatic breach.

Usually it’s smaller and dumber than that.

It’s scattered policies.
It’s unclear vendor responsibility.
It’s missing documentation.
It’s staff doing their best with no real system.

That’s why “we care about compliance” is not the same as being ready.

The hard part is not saying HIPAA matters.
The hard part is knowing:
- where you actually stand
- what your biggest gaps are
- what needs attention first

That’s the bar.

---

## Post 3 — Category point of view
The compliance market has a weird blind spot.

If you’re a bigger company, there are plenty of tools.
If you’re a tiny practice, you’re often stuck between:
- enterprise software that feels built for another planet
- or manual checklists that do nothing once real work starts

That middle gap is real.

Small healthcare practices still need to be ready.
They still need documentation.
They still need a way to understand risk without hiring a full compliance team.

That’s why we’re building ShieldRA AI the way we are.

Not broader.
Sharper.
More usable.
Closer to how small practices actually operate.

## 7. Community Post Angles

Use these in Reddit, Facebook groups, association communities, or comment threads.

### Angle 1
What part of HIPAA readiness is most annoying for smaller practices right now: vendor paperwork, policy documentation, staff training, or just figuring out what actually matters first?

### Angle 2
For operators in small practices: what usually slows down HIPAA cleanup the most, lack of time, unclear ownership, or too many moving pieces?

### Angle 3
A lot of compliance tools seem built for teams with more time and more headcount than small practices actually have. Curious what people are using today that genuinely helps.

Rule: ask, contribute, discuss. Do not hard pitch on the first touch.
