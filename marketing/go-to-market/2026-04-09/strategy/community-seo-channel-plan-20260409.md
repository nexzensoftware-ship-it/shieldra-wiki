# ShieldRA community, listings, and SEO channel plan — 2026-04-09
**Owner:** Hodor  
**Purpose:** Answer the obvious next questions after outbound, where else should ShieldRA show up, and what should the SEO motion actually be?

---

## Executive take

Yes, ShieldRA should expand beyond direct outreach.

But not by spraying links all over the internet like a desperate raccoon.

The right move is:
1. **Use Reddit for message testing and language mining**
2. **Use Facebook groups for operator-level discussion and trust**
3. **Use LinkedIn as the main public credibility layer**
4. **List selectively on real startup/software pages**
5. **Build backlinks through partners, guest content, and useful assets, not junk directory spam**
6. **Publish once per week, consistently, and repurpose hard**

---

## 1. Reddit: yes, but as a research + trust channel first

### Best-fit subreddits that surfaced in current search
- `r/Dentistry`
- `r/Dentists`
- `r/healthIT`
- `r/msp`
- `r/sysadmin`

### What Reddit is good for
- seeing how practice owners and operators actually talk about HIPAA pain
- finding recurring objections, especially around cost, training, BAAs, shared logins, backups, and admin burden
- replying helpfully on existing threads
- testing educational posts without hard-selling

### What Reddit is bad for
- direct link drops
- obvious promotion
- fake founder theater

### Recommended Reddit motion
For the first 30 days:
- **2 comment sessions per week**
- answer real threads where HIPAA, dental operations, or small-practice IT pain already exists
- save phrases people repeat and feed that back into homepage copy, outreach, and blog titles

### Good post angles
- “How are smaller practices handling HIPAA without turning it into a full-time job?”
- “What usually breaks first in front-desk HIPAA workflows?”
- “What do small dental practices actually use for annual HIPAA reviews and vendor tracking?”

### Rule
Reddit should be treated as a **trust and insight channel**, not the main acquisition engine.

## 2. Facebook groups: better than Reddit for operator access

This is probably the stronger community lane for ShieldRA.

### Why
Practice admins, office managers, and smaller private-practice operators are far more likely to hang out in Facebook groups than in startup-land channels.

### Current-search examples that surfaced
- **Dental Office Managers**
- HIPAA-focused small-practice discussion groups
- dental admin / OSHA-HIPAA discussion threads inside office-manager groups

### What to do there
- join the relevant groups with a real founder/operator profile
- spend the first 1 to 2 weeks commenting usefully instead of posting promos
- answer questions on training, risk assessments, shared logins, vendor management, and front-desk workflows
- only post original educational content after there is some visible participation history

### Good Facebook post angles
- short checklist posts
- “3 things small practices miss in HIPAA reviews”
- “front desk + vendor + backups = where small-practice HIPAA gets messy fast”
- “if you had to tighten one HIPAA workflow this quarter, which one would it be?”

### Bad Facebook move
Joining groups and immediately posting “try our software” is how you get ignored or booted.

## 3. LinkedIn stays the main public channel

If we have to pick one public channel to take seriously, it is still LinkedIn.

### Why
- founders, consultants, HIPAA advisors, and healthcare IT partners are there
- partner/referral opportunities are easier to create there
- content has a longer half-life than X
- it supports both social proof and direct outreach

### Recommended cadence
Per week:
- **3 posts**
- **5 to 10 meaningful comments** on healthcare ops, dental ops, HIPAA, and small-practice IT posts
- **5 to 15 targeted connection/outreach touches**

### Best LinkedIn content types
- operator pain breakdowns
- lessons from common dental HIPAA gaps
- practical checklists
- “what small practices think HIPAA means vs what actually creates risk”
- posts tied to new blog content or downloadable assets

## 4. Startup pages and directories: yes, but prioritize the right ones

There are startup pages worth listing on.

Just do **not** go full low-rent directory goblin.

### Do now
These are worth setting up soon, even before heavy traction:
- **Crunchbase**
- **Wellfound**
- **Product Hunt** prep page / launch planning
- **BetaList** if positioning is still early-stage enough to fit
- **LinkedIn Company Page** if not already tightened up

### Do after first real users, pilots, or strong proof
These matter more once there are legitimate reviews and product detail worth comparing:
- **G2**
- **Capterra**
- **GetApp**
- **TrustRadius**

### Maybe later, only if time exists
- AlternativeTo
- SaaSHub / SaaSWorthy style directories
- niche software directories where healthcare buyers actually browse

### My take
The best near-term listing stack is:
1. Crunchbase
2. Wellfound
3. Product Hunt prep
4. G2/Capterra only after real review motion exists

If ShieldRA has no users yet, a half-empty G2 profile is not a flex. It just looks unfinished.

## 5. Backlink strategy: yes, but earned links only

Backlinks matter.

Buying garbage links or blasting 80 directories is stupid and usually counterproductive.

### Best backlink sources for ShieldRA

#### A. Partner backlinks
Highest-value lane.
Targets:
- HIPAA consultants
- dental/medical MSPs
- healthcare cybersecurity firms
- compliance advisors
- medical billing / practice-ops partners

How to earn links:
- co-branded webinar
- guest article exchange
- partner resource page mention
- joint checklist
- referral partner page

#### B. Linkable assets on the ShieldRA site
Create pages people can actually reference.
Best asset ideas:
- Dental HIPAA Risk Assessment Checklist
- Small Practice HIPAA Vendor Checklist
- Front Desk HIPAA Readiness Checklist
- Annual HIPAA Review Checklist for Independent Practices
- “What vendors need a BAA?” explainer for small practices

These are much more linkable than generic product pages.

#### C. Guest posts and podcasts
Good targets:
- dental operations blogs
- healthcare IT newsletters
- small-practice cybersecurity blogs
- compliance-focused podcasts or webinars

#### D. Local and industry citations
Worth doing if relevant:
- Colorado healthcare / dental business directories
- state or local dental society resource pages
- chamber and healthcare-business directories where the listing is real, not spammy

### Internal-linking rule
Every blog post should link to:
- the homepage
- the Free HIPAA Risk Assessment CTA
- one relevant checklist or resource page
- one related blog post

### External-linking rule
Link out to credible authorities when helpful:
- HHS / OCR
- ADA
- trusted security/compliance resources

That helps credibility and keeps the content from sounding invented.

## 6. Blog cadence: once a week is fine

Yes. **Once a week is a solid cadence** for ShieldRA right now.

That is enough if the posts are:
- specific
- useful
- internally linked
- repurposed into other channels
- paired with outreach and backlink work

### What once-weekly should look like
Each week publish **1 strong post**, then turn it into:
- 2 to 3 LinkedIn posts
- 1 Facebook group discussion angle
- 1 Reddit question/comment angle
- 1 short email or partner outreach hook
- 1 checklist snippet or carousel

### Better than “just blog weekly”
Use a simple monthly structure:
- **Week 1:** operator pain post
- **Week 2:** checklist / how-to post
- **Week 3:** vendor / workflow / BAA post
- **Week 4:** compliance lesson from an enforcement trend, workflow failure, or common misconception

### My take
One great post per week beats four forgettable SEO posts no one trusts.

## 8. Channel priority order

If time is limited, do this in order:

1. **LinkedIn**
2. **Facebook groups**
3. **Partner backlink outreach**
4. **Weekly blog + repurposing**
5. **Selective startup listings**
6. **Reddit**

### Why Reddit is lower
Useful, yes.
Primary growth engine, no.
Too much moderation risk, too little control, and too easy to look promotional.

## Final call

The clean answer is:
- **yes** to Reddit, but carefully
- **yes** to Facebook groups, and honestly that is the better bet
- **yes** to startup listings, but only the real ones
- **yes** to backlinks, but earn them through partner content and useful assets
- **yes** to a once-a-week blog cadence

If ShieldRA does those five things with discipline, it will look real fast.
If it just blasts links around, it will look like every other compliance tool begging for attention.
