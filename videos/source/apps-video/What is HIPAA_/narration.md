## Scene 2 — Title (5–13s)
If your business touches health data, one law decides whether you keep operating — or get fined into the ground.

## Scene 3 — Definition (13–30s)
HIPAA. The Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act. A nineteen-ninety-six U.S. federal law, signed by President Bill Clinton, that protects sensitive patient health information from being shared without consent.

## Scene 4 — PHI (30–48s)
At the center of HIPAA is something called Protected Health Information — or PHI. Names, diagnoses, treatment records, billing data, even an IP address tied to a patient. If it can identify someone and it lives in a healthcare context, HIPAA covers it.

## Scene 5 — Rules (48–65s)
HIPAA stands on three pillars. The Privacy Rule — who can see PHI. The Security Rule — how PHI is stored and transmitted. And the Breach Notification Rule — what you have to do, and how fast, when something goes wrong.

## Scene 6 — Importance (65–88s)
And things go wrong constantly. Healthcare data breaches have nearly tripled since two-thousand-eighteen. In twenty-twenty-four alone, more than two hundred and seventy-five million patient records were exposed in the United States — the highest number ever recorded. Healthcare is now the most-targeted industry on the planet.

## Scene 7 — Penalties (88–105s)
The fines reflect that. A single HIPAA violation can cost up to one-point-nine million dollars. Repeat or willful neglect can lead to criminal charges — and yes, jail time. This isn't theoretical. The Office for Civil Rights enforces it, and they are auditing.

## Scene 8 — Breaches (105–122s)
And when OCR audits, no one is too big. Anthem. Premera. Excellus. Even Memorial Healthcare. Settlements in the tens of millions. The lesson is simple: scale is not a defense. If you handle PHI, the rules apply to you.

## Scene 9 — Who (122–139s)
HIPAA reaches far beyond hospitals. Insurance carriers. Telehealth apps. Billing companies. Cloud vendors. Pharmacies. Even the analytics tool that touches one patient record — they're all on the hook as covered entities or business associates.

## Scene 10 — Patient Rights (139–152s)
And HIPAA gives patients six core rights. The right to access their records. To request corrections. To know who's seen them. To restrict sharing. To get a copy. And to file a complaint — directly to the regulator.
