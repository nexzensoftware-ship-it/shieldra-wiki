## Scene 2 — Hook (10–25s)
What is SOC 2? It's the compliance standard every SaaS founder hears about — and almost nobody can explain in one sentence. Let's fix that.

## Scene 3 — Definition (25–65s)
SOC 2 is a report. Not a certification, not a stamp — a report. Issued by an independent C-P-A firm, it proves that your company protects customer data the way you say you do. It was built by the American Institute of C-P-As, and today it's the de facto trust standard for any business that handles someone else's data in the cloud.

## Scene 4 — Why (65–100s)
And it's not optional anymore. The average data breach now costs four-point-eight-eight million dollars. Eighty-two percent of enterprise buyers require a current SOC 2 before they'll even sign. No SOC 2, no enterprise deal — full stop. The companies that get one close faster, lose fewer questionnaires, and unlock contracts that were closed to them yesterday.

## Scene 5 — TSC (100–145s)
SOC 2 is built on five Trust Services Criteria. Security is the only one that's required — every SOC 2 report includes it. The other four are optional, scoped to what your business actually does. Availability — uptime and disaster recovery. Confidentiality — protecting sensitive business data. Processing Integrity — making sure your systems do what they're supposed to. And Privacy — handling personal information the way your privacy notice says you will.

## Scene 6 — Types (145–175s)
There are two flavors. SOC 2 Type One is a snapshot — does your control design look right today? It's faster to get, but enterprise buyers rarely accept it. SOC 2 Type Two is the gold standard. It proves your controls operated effectively over a window of time — usually six to twelve months. That's the one that closes deals.

## Scene 7 — Audit (175–205s)
Getting there is a journey. Readiness — map your controls, find the gaps. Remediation — write the policies, turn on M-F-A, wire the alerts. Observation — the audit window, where controls have to run continuously. Fieldwork — the auditor tests your evidence. And finally, the report — signed, sealed, and renewed every twelve months.
